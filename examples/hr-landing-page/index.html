Questions

The ones we get on every intro call.

Are you a PEO?

No. A PEO becomes the co-employer of record and takes your payroll and benefits onto their contracts. We work alongside whatever you already use — your payroll provider, your broker, your carriers — and you keep the relationships and the data. If you're currently on a PEO and want out, we've run that exit before.

Do you replace an in-house HR person?

Under about 150 employees, we usually are the HR team, and it costs less than a senior generalist. Past that, most companies want someone in the building — so we build the function, hire your first HR lead, and hand it over. That's the Build & transition engagement.

Can you give us legal advice?

No, and you should be wary of any HR firm that says otherwise. We identify risk, document properly, and keep you inside standard practice. When something needs a legal opinion — a contested termination, a discrimination claim, a non-compete question — we brief your employment counsel and work with them.

We hire in several states. Is that a problem?

It's the normal case now. Each state you employ someone in brings its own leave rules, final-pay timing, posting requirements, and handbook addenda. We map it per state during the audit and keep it current as you add locations.

How fast can you start?

The audit usually begins within two weeks of signing, and takes two weeks to complete. If you're in the middle of something urgent — an investigation, a layoff, an open enrollment deadline — say so on the intro call and we'll tell you honestly whether we can take it on right now.

What does it take from our side?

About two hours in week one for access and context, then roughly an hour a month with leadership. Your managers get a place to send questions. Nobody on your team has to become a part-time HR administrator.
